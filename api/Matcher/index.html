Matcher

The grouping algorithm. Pure: no store, no clock, no Roblox globals — it takes candidates and a policy and returns groups, which is what makes match quality testable without a queue around it.

Candidates are processed oldest-first, and each unused one becomes an anchor. For an anchor, a subset-sum walk over party sizes finds the best combination that fills the match, where "best" is the policy's score: smallest rating spread, then shortest total wait. Parties enter the walk as single indivisible candidates, so a party is never split.

A group at targetSize commits immediately. A smaller one is only accepted once the anchor has waited fallbackAfter seconds, and then the largest viable size down to minSize wins — so a queue with five players waiting for a six-player match eventually plays five-a-side rather than waiting forever.

The rating window widens with the anchor's wait, not each candidate's. Since anchors are oldest-first, the player who has waited longest searches the widest, and a lone high-rated outlier who cannot be matched never blocks the compatible players behind it.

Which full match wins

The walk stops at the first combination that reaches targetSize rather than continuing to look for a tighter one. Since candidates are visited oldest-first, that means the longest-waiting players get in, and rating spread is already bounded by the window every candidate had to pass. The alternative — scanning all of them to shave a few rating points — costs a full pass per anchor on every scan of every lane, which is not a trade worth making on a server that is also running a game.

Rating spread across every individual member, then total queue time. Spread is measured per member rather than per ticket so a party with a wide internal skill gap is correctly penalized.

Returns a spread of 0 when no candidate carries ratings, which makes the tie-break on queue time the whole ordering — the right policy for an unrated queue.
