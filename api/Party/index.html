Party

Cross-server parties: a group of players who queue together and are never split across matches.

:::tip Do you need this? Probably not. Matchmaker:enqueueGroup({ userIds }) already queues any set of players as one indivisible ticket, and if your parties form in a single lobby they can live in a plain table on that server.

This exists for parties that must survive members being on different servers: join-friend landing someone in another server because your own is full, inviting a player who is not in your lobby yet, or a party regrouping after a match scatters everyone across lobbies. If none of those describe your game, use enqueueGroup instead.

A Matchmaker builds this on first access to queue.parties, and it writes nothing to the store until you call something on it. :::

Membership is enforced by a single-slot index per user, written with compare-and-set. A player belongs to at most one party, and two servers racing to add the same player will see exactly one of them win — which is the whole problem, since the two servers cannot see each other.

Accepting an invite is a two-phase reservation: a short-lived Reserved slot is taken first, the party record is updated second, and the slot is promoted to Active only once the join actually succeeded. A crash between the phases leaves a reservation that expires in 20 seconds rather than a player wedged in a party that never got them.

While a party is queued its membership is frozen — invites, kicks, leaves, leader transfers and disbands all fail with PartyLocked — so the roster the matchmaker matched cannot change underneath it. An abandoned queue lock repairs itself on the next read rather than freezing the party forever. 

local parties = Muster.Party.new(store)

local party = Muster.Result.unwrap(parties:create(leaderUserId))
parties:invite(leaderUserId, friendUserId)

-- On the friend's server, possibly a different one entirely:
local invites = Muster.Result.unwrap(parties:listInvites(friendUserId))
parties:acceptInvite(friendUserId, invites[1].id)
